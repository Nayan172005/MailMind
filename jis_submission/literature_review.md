## 2. Research Gap and Positioning of MailMind

### 2.1 The Identified Research Gap
While modern literature has extensively addressed **open-domain RAG** (Lewis et al. [16]; Gao et al. [17]) and **large-scale enterprise search** (Wang et al. [3]; Bendersky et al. [4]), personal email collections present distinct information retrieval challenges that fall through the cracks of existing paradigms:
1. **The Semantic-Lexical Tension in Personal Email:** Personal correspondence contains high-frequency specific alphanumeric entities (e.g., job profile IDs, room numbers, course codes like `CSE3002`, deadline dates) alongside conversational queries expressing high-level semantic intents (e.g., *"When should I submit my project documentation?"*). Dense retrieval models fail on novel acronyms, while lexical search fails on semantic paraphrasing.
2. **Multi-Message Redundancy and Temporal Evolution:** A placement drive or course announcement rarely consists of a single isolated email; it evolves across an initial circular, amendments, shortlists, and venue schedules. Standard chunk-level retrieval floods LLM context windows with multiple fragments of the same message or out-of-date circulars.
3. **Retrieval Freshness under Streaming Ingestion:** Email is continuously arriving. Existing personal RAG prototypes require stopping and restarting local applications or rebuilding monolithic vector stores to reflect newly arrived messages, creating a barrier to practical adoption.
4. **The Criticality of Abstention in Personal PIM:** Unlike general search where speculative generation may be tolerable, hallucinating a placement interview venue or examination date carries severe real-world consequences. A personal email assistant must reliably abstain when evidence is missing.

### 2.2 MailMind's Scientific Positioning
MailMind does not claim to introduce a novel fundamental transformer backbone or a new foundational vector index algorithm. Instead, it contributes an **empirically audited, vertically integrated hybrid RAG framework** tailored for personal email information management. Specifically, it establishes:
- A multi-stage retrieval architecture combining dense FAISS search, BM25 lexical retrieval, Reciprocal Rank Fusion ($k=60$), query intent/entity analysis, and a 6-signal metadata reranker.
- A lightweight, CPU-efficient temporal recency decay function that balances semantic relevance with email freshness.
- An atomic, thread-safe, signature-based cache invalidation mechanism that enables real-time ingestion from Gmail Pub/Sub push webhooks without Streamlit UI restarts.
- A rigorous, multi-pillar evaluation protocol separating development from frozen test sets (50 queries, 105 relevance judgments across 8 categories), measuring retrieval recall, RAG groundedness, citation validity, and BiLSTM email prioritization.
